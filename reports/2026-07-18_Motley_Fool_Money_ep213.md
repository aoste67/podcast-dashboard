## Macro & Sector Themes
*   **Retirement Spending Patterns:** Evidence suggests retirees do not increase spending by the full rate of inflation over time (e.g., spending may only rise 1% when inflation is 3%), following a "go-go, slow-go, no-go" lifecycle.
*   **Safe Withdrawal Rates (SWR):** Critique of the traditional 4% rule; research suggests initial SWR could increase to 5%–6.5% if spending flexibility is incorporated.
*   **Financial Planning Methodology:** Criticism of Monte Carlo projections for using binary "success/failure" metrics that fail to account for the nuance of discretionary vs. essential spending.
*   **Healthcare Volatility:** While most retirees avoid catastrophic costs, a minority (5-20%) face extreme late-life long-term care expenses.

## Actionable Takeaways
*   **Adjust Withdrawal Rates:** Consider increasing initial withdrawal rates above 4% if you have the flexibility to reduce discretionary spending in later years.
*   **Segment Expenses:** Divide retirement budget into "essential" (fixed) and "discretionary" (flexible). Higher SWRs are sustainable when a larger portion of the portfolio covers flexible expenses.
*   **Secure Lifetime Income:** Prioritize covering all essential expenses with guaranteed lifetime income to reduce behavioral risk and enable more aggressive portfolio spending.
*   **Inflation Buffer as Insurance:** Maintain traditional inflation-adjusted projections in planning not for spending, but as an implicit "slush fund" to hedge against late-life long-term care costs.
